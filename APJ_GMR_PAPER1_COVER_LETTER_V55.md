Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, "Testing Baryonic Geometric-Response Information on SPARC Rotation Curves," for publication in The Astrophysical Journal.

This manuscript tests whether baryonic geometric-response invariants add predictive information beyond a simple square-root response on the public SPARC rotation-curve sample. The analysis uses a fixed GMR implementation with no galaxy-by-galaxy retuning on the full 175-galaxy sample and on a held-out 151-galaxy subset.

The central result is a constraint rather than a superiority claim. A pure globally fitted square-root control slightly outperforms full GMR on the primary equal-weight RMSE metric, so the manuscript does not claim that the full GMR architecture is the best fitting model. Instead, it reports a reproducible limit on the tested invariant architecture: the cumulative state variable improves over a tested local-state replacement, but the full baryonic-invariant structure does not improve over the simpler square-root response on the present aggregate benchmark.

GMR remains competitive relative to baryons-only, MOND, and reduced NFW on the same aggregate metric, while reduced NFW retains the median-residual and per-galaxy win-rate advantage and reduced ISO/Burkert remain stronger empirical halo prescriptions overall. The manuscript also reports relation-level diagnostics and explicit failure-mode analysis in low-mass, low-surface-brightness dwarf systems.

The scope is deliberately limited. The paper does not claim a complete dark-sector theory, a relativistic action closure, cosmological implementation, cluster result, or strong-field result. Repository metadata and archive DOI will be inserted once the public release is created.
